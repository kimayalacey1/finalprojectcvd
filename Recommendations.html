From Cardiovascular Data to Public Health Action

Cardiovascular mortality disparities highlight the need for comprehensive public health strategies that prioritize prevention, early detection, treatment, and equitable access to care.

Addressing cardiovascular health requires collaboration among healthcare providers, public health professionals, policymakers, and community organizations. By implementing evidence-informed strategies at multiple levels, communities can work toward reducing preventable cardiovascular deaths and improving health outcomes.

The Public Health Approach

A systematic approach to cardiovascular health allows public health professionals to identify disparities, investigate contributing factors, develop interventions, and evaluate outcomes.

01

Define the Problem

Identify populations and communities experiencing disproportionate cardiovascular mortality.

02

Identify Risk Factors

Examine behavioral, environmental, socioeconomic, and healthcare-related contributors to cardiovascular disease.

03

Develop Solutions

Implement prevention programs, community screening, healthcare interventions, and policy strategies.

04

Evaluate & Improve

Monitor outcomes, assess effectiveness, and expand successful public health interventions.

Prevention Strategies

Cardiovascular disease prevention requires interventions across multiple stages of disease progression. Primary, secondary, and tertiary prevention strategies can help reduce risk, improve early detection, and prevent complications.

PREVENTION LEVEL 01

Primary Prevention

Prevent cardiovascular disease before it develops.

♡ Heart-Healthy Nutrition

Promote access to affordable fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lower-sodium food options.

♡ Physical Activity

Encourage regular exercise through walking programs, recreation spaces, and community fitness initiatives.

♡ Tobacco Prevention

Expand tobacco prevention education and smoking cessation programs.

♡ Health Education

Improve awareness of hypertension, diabetes, cholesterol, cardiovascular risk, and prevention.

Secondary Prevention

Identify cardiovascular risk factors early.

PREVENTION LEVEL 03

Tertiary Prevention

Reduce complications among individuals living with cardiovascular disease.

♡ Chronic Disease Management

Strengthen access to ongoing cardiovascular treatment and long-term disease management.

♡ Medication Access

Support medication adherence through education, affordable prescriptions, and patient assistance.

♡ Cardiac Rehabilitation

Expand access to cardiac rehabilitation and recovery services.

♡ Care Coordination

Improve communication between primary care, specialists, hospitals, and community-based services.

COMMUNITY & HEALTH EQUITY

Addressing the Root Causes of Cardiovascular Disparities

Cardiovascular health is influenced by more than individual behaviors. Social, economic, environmental, and structural conditions can shape access to prevention, treatment, and resources. Public health strategies should prioritize the communities experiencing the greatest burden.

Community Health Workers

Employ trusted community health workers to connect residents with screening, education, healthcare, and social resources.

Food Access & Nutrition Equity

Support food access initiatives, nutrition assistance, and healthy food availability in communities experiencing food insecurity.

Healthy Neighborhoods

Promote safe walking spaces, parks, recreation facilities, and environments that support physical activity.

Equitable Healthcare Access

Address barriers related to affordability, transportation, insurance coverage, healthcare availability, and culturally responsive care.

THE FUTURE OF CARDIOVASCULAR HEALTH

Building Stronger, Healthier Communities

Cardiovascular health is influenced by more than individual behavior. Geographic disparities, social determinants of health, healthcare access, and structural barriers all contribute to population health outcomes.

Addressing cardiovascular mortality requires a coordinated public health approach that prioritizes prevention, early detection, equitable treatment, community engagement, and evidence-informed policy.

By investing in communities and reducing barriers to care, we can move toward a future where cardiovascular health opportunities are accessible to everyone.
